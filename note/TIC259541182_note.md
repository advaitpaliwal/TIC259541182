# A 1.15 Earth-radius Planet Candidate on a 1.00-day Orbit around the Nearby M Dwarf TIC 259541182

Advait Paliwal
Independent researcher, Sterling Heights, MI 48314, USA
advaitpaliwal@gmail.com

## Abstract

I report a transit-like signal on TIC 259541182 (2MASS J04470045−4836020), an M dwarf at 45.6 pc. The signal appears in TESS full-frame-image photometry from six sectors spanning 2018–2025. A transit fit gives a period of 1.0044652 ± 0.0000009 d, a depth of 787 ± 69 ppm and a duration of 0.79 h, implying a radius of 1.15 ± 0.07 R⊕. The star is not a TOI or CTOI, and the SPOC pipeline lists no threshold-crossing event for it. The signal is present in raw and corrected flux and in six independent extractions. Odd and even depths are consistent, and there is no secondary eclipse. Difference images favour the target over every Gaia source within 60″ by Δχ² ≥ 150. With pixel exclusions and Gaia companion limits, TRICERATOPS gives FPP = 0.034 ± 0.002. This is above the 0.015 validation threshold, so the candidate awaits high-resolution imaging.

## Star and search

TIC 259541182 (Stassun et al. 2019) has T = 11.95, Teff = 3457 ± 157 K, R★ = 0.392 ± 0.012 R☉ and M★ = 0.383 ± 0.020 M☉ (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023). SIMBAD lists it as the proper-motion star LEHPM 3836. ExoFOP has no imaging or spectroscopic follow-up for it. The star has no TESS 2-minute data. I searched TESS-SPOC (Caldwell et al. 2020) and QLP (Huang et al. 2020a, 2020b; Kunimoto et al. 2021) full-frame light curves of nearby cool dwarfs lacking 2-minute data. The search used Transit Least Squares (Hippke & Heller 2019) over 1–12 d. Before any false-positive analysis, each detection had to pass three gates: the dip must appear in raw as well as corrected flux, the two pipelines must agree on its depth, and difference images must place it on the target.

## Transit fit

I fitted the native-cadence light curves (1800 s in Sectors 4–5, 600 s in 31–32 and 200 s in 97–98) with batman (Kreidberg 2015) and emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013). The model integrates over each exposure, assumes a circular orbit, and uses a TIC stellar-density prior and quadratic limb darkening from Claret (2017). It gives P = 1.0044652 ± 0.0000009 d, T0 = 2460968.5979 ± 0.0011 BJD_TDB, Rp/R★ = 0.0270 ± 0.0014, a/R★ = 7.82 ± 0.10, b = 0.64 (+0.06/−0.08), a depth of 787 (+69/−68) ppm and T14 = 0.79 ± 0.06 h (Figure 1). This implies Rp = 1.15 ± 0.07 R⊕, a = 0.0143 ± 0.0003 AU, an insolation of 98 ± 24 S⊕ and Teq = 875 ± 40 K.

## Vetting

Combined depths are 676 ± 56 ppm (TESS-SPOC), 571 ± 46 ppm (QLP), 724 ± 86 ppm (TARS), 738 ± 86 ppm (eleanor-style aperture) and 803 ± 87 ppm (TGLC aperture; Han & Brandt 2023). Raw and corrected flux give consistent depths in every sector with data, with raw significance of 8.2σ (SPOC) and 10.5σ (QLP). Odd and even depths agree within 1.1σ in QLP and within 0.9σ in SPOC, so the light curve folded at 2P shows no alternating eclipse depths. The phase-0.5 depth is 8 ± 45 ppm in QLP. One extraction, TGLC PSF photometry in Sector 4, shows a 4.6σ odd–even difference; the other extractions do not. Injected 560 ppm transits are recovered at 85–98% depending on cadence. Same-camera, same-CCD comparison stars show no dip at the transit times: none of 35 folds exceeds |S/N| = 3, against S/N = 15 for the target. Transit times fall near momentum dumps no more often than shifted ephemerides do.

Difference images from TESScut (Brasseur et al. 2019), fitted with the TESS PRF, place the in-transit flux deficit on the target in all six sectors. Each of the three Gaia sources within 60″ is disfavoured relative to the target by a summed Δχ² of at least 150.6, and none is preferred in any sector.

## False-positive probability

I averaged TRICERATOPS (Giacalone & Dressing 2020; Giacalone et al. 2021) over ten runs of 10⁶ draws each. The excluded neighbours were removed, and companion limits from Gaia astrometry and imaging via MOLUSC (Wood et al. 2021) were applied in the same runs. The result is FPP = 0.0335 ± 0.0023 and NFPP = 3 × 10⁻¹⁰. Almost all of the FPP (0.033) comes from a planet orbiting an unresolved bound companion. MOLUSC cannot exclude companions inside about 1 AU, and the star has no high-resolution imaging. The calculation assumed 30-minute exposures and approximated the QLP aperture. Collier et al. (2026) find that small validated planets in their imaging sample needed contrast curves to pass, so I make no validation claim.

## Summary

TIC 259541182.01 is a candidate ultra-short-period planet, close to Earth in size, around a nearby M dwarf. With K ≈ 11.7 and a predicted radial-velocity semi-amplitude of a few m s⁻¹ for a rocky composition, it would make a good target for high-resolution imaging and precise radial velocities. Code and data are at https://github.com/advaitpaliwal/TIC259541182.
